# FieldShift data-gap resource catalog

Research checked: 15 November 2026. This is a source-selection document, not evidence that these datasets are integrated or that pilot-area extracts have been validated. The initial research checked product/access pages without acquiring raster archives. The subsequent [SoilGrids pilot milestone](SOILGRIDS_PILOT_MILESTONE.md) acquires a separate, versioned SOIL-01 raster package; other catalog entries remain source selections. Priorities and proposed uses below are implementation recommendations.

## Selection and access

Prefer openly reusable scientific data with published provider terms. Open data, public-domain data, and open-source software are different categories. A free account can be compatible with open data, but account-protected acquisition must happen during authorized preprocessing, not through credentials embedded in our zero-auth frontend. Keep original license notices and attribution alongside derived data; an application code license does not replace a data license.

NASA describes its Earth science data as fully and openly available without restrictions in its [science and applications policy statement](https://www.nasa.gov/goddard/earth-science-projects-division/espd-science-and-applications/). The NASA products below are selected under that open-data basis; retain product-specific citations, versions, quality information, and any accompanying notices. [AppEEARS requires Earthdata login](https://nsidc.org/data/user-resources/help-center/how-extract-point-and-area-data-samples-using-appeears). It supports extracting point/area samples rather than requiring us to download global archives.

These selections do not certify hackathon eligibility. Separately confirm that at least one used NASA source belongs to the official challenge Resources list.

## The supplied Flood-MAR-Kaz paper

[Skakova, Utepov, Arystanova and Yessenov, *Flood-MAR-Kaz*](https://www.mdpi.com/2225-1154/14/11/237), DOI **10.3390/cli14110237**, is useful as a methodological reference. Its GPM, MOD16, ERA5-Land and GRACE sources are included above; extract Philippine observations from those providers rather than reusing Kazakhstan regional summaries.

Adopt its distinction between supported, unresolved and excluded evidence and its separation of regional screening from field verification. It does not validate recharge performance or establish irrigation-water availability. Its station records, project UAV/LiDAR terrain and flood simulations are not an openly archived dataset; access is subject to author/source restrictions. Do not bundle those project outputs as open data. Published article reuse follows the applicable article license and [publisher terms](https://www.mdpi.com/about/termsofuse); third-party data rights remain separate.

## Public references that are not approved for bundling

- **BSWM Philippine Soil Series** has a [public shapefile download listing](https://www.bswm.da.gov.ph/download/philippine-soil-series/). The full page timed out during this review, so archive-level notices, survey dates, map scale, attributes and redistribution conditions were not inspected. Useful local validation lead; not yet an approved import. A general website footer is insufficient to establish the terms of every attached dataset.
- **HazardHunterPH** provides public [hazard screening and FAQs](https://hazardhunter.georisk.gov.ph/faqs), but its [terms](https://hazardhunter.georisk.gov.ph/terms-of-use) include restrictions on reuse. Link technicians to the official service for local review; do not scrape or redistribute its layers on the assumption that public viewing grants that right.
- **Global Flood Database** was not selected as the default open import: its [provider README](https://storage.googleapis.com/gfd_metadata/README_GFD.pdf) states a noncommercial license. Prefer the explicitly licensed JRC record for an openly reusable candidate, subject to its database obligations.

## Gaps these sources cannot close on their own

- Field drainage, canal schedules, irrigation access, plow pans and compaction require field/irrigation-office evidence. Texture and terrain are only partial indicators.
- Field pH, nutrients and phosphorus fixation require appropriate soil analyses and local interpretation. SoilGrids does not establish a measured phosphorus-fixation classification; pH alone cannot validate the current derived risk rule.
- Crop-specific planting windows, maturity, cultivar suitability, disease breaks and nitrogen-fixation amounts still require verified agronomic references and local review. Satellite vegetation and market statistics do not validate existing crop constants.
- Local buyer prices, available labor, input costs, farmer history and goals need current user/extension inputs. Report statistical context separately.
- Annual RUSLE tonnes/ha/year require defensible R, K, LS, C and P factors with compatible units and calibration. These sources can inform terrain/cover and rainfall context; they do not validate our existing synthetic factors, NDVI-to-C mapping, or management practice assumptions. Leave quantitative erosion unavailable until justified.
- Low NDVI has several causes; annual NDVI trend is a vegetation signal, not a direct soil degradation diagnosis. Clouds, seasonal coverage and land-cover change must be assessed.

## Proposed review milestones

1. **Data integrity:** remove numeric substitutes for absent readings, reject malformed/empty responses, version affected caches, and show provisional plan status when critical evidence is missing. Keep missingness distinct from valid zeros and preserve synthetic fixtures as explicitly labeled demonstrations.
2. **Real pilot packages:** start with SOIL-01, TERRAIN-01 and VEG-01 for the four hubs, alongside calculated CLIMATE-01 outputs. Add new versioned derived-data files rather than silently replacing immutable samples. Sample named points or explicit areas; impose coverage/distance rules instead of assigning a municipal profile to arbitrary distant coordinates. Stop for review.
3. **Context and validation:** add ECON-01 and RAIN-02, then land cover and finer HLS imagery as needed. Review flood/ET context separately before modifying scoring or scenario rules. Keep unsupported metrics unavailable.

For every imported package record source URL/DOI, version, license and attribution, actual observation period, actual retrieval time, coordinates/bounds, grid and effective scale, depth, units/scaling, nodata convention, QA/valid-observation counts, uncertainty, processing method/version, checksum and review status. Validate external and packaged data with Zod, retain reproducible extraction scripts, and keep credentials outside distributed artifacts. Use existing seven-day JSON cache behavior for applicable live requests; label offline extracts by their observation period rather than claiming that cache freshness makes observations current.

Implementation acceptance must include numerical spot checks against provider values, unit/depth tests, empty/partial/no-coverage responses, cloud/nodata filtering, stale caches, and all four pilot locations. Milestone 2 now has an implemented review stop: see [REAL_SOURCE_INTEGRATION_MILESTONE.md](REAL_SOURCE_INTEGRATION_MILESTONE.md) for exact coverage, observed verification, remaining gaps and touched files. Milestone 3 remains pending.
